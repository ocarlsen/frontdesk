FrontDesk

An open-source inbox agent. It reads your Gmail, ranks what actually needs you, says why in one line, and has a reply drafted for the threads that want one. It runs on your computer, with the model you choose.

Try the live demo Privacy policy

  • Local-firstNo server, no account, no analytics. Your mail stays on your machine.
  • Read-only by defaultArchive and drafts need a second, separate permission. It never sends.
  • MeasuredLabeled test set and a 32-attack prompt-injection suite, scored per model.
  • Bring your own modelOne small adapter per vendor; switching is a setting.

How it works

  1. Sync. A read-only Gmail token pulls recent threads and normalizes them: charset fixes, quoted history stripped, inline images embedded for display.
  2. Triage. Each thread goes to the model as quoted data it cannot break out of, and comes back in a fixed format that the code validates again. Every ranking records its model, prompt version, tokens and cost.
  3. Act. You read the thread rendered like Gmail, edit the suggested reply, and save it as a Gmail draft, or archive the thread with one click and undo.

How I know it works

I measure it on 80 labeled synthetic threads and 32 prompt-injection attacks: mail that tries to talk the model into misfiling it, or into putting the attacker's words into my reply.

The default is the cheapest model that clears every safety floor: never file a thread that needed you as ignore, and never carry an attacker's text into a reply. Sonnet 5 is cheaper but copied a forged payment commitment into one reply, so it fails that floor. The full report, with confidence intervals, is in the repository.

What it doesn't claim. Prompt injection is not solved. FrontDesk limits what a malicious email can do (it cannot send, run code in the page, or use tools), and the attack suite measures how often one still sways a ranking or a draft.

Privacy policy

Last updated October 28, 2026

What FrontDesk is

FrontDesk is software you run on your own computer. There is no FrontDesk server, no FrontDesk account and no analytics. When you sign in with Google, you grant access to the copy of FrontDesk running on your computer; the "FrontDesk" name on Google's sign-in screen is the author's Google Cloud project, and your mail never passes through the author or any FrontDesk server.

What it reads from Google

With the read-only permission, FrontDesk reads the Gmail threads that match a search you set (by default, your inbox from the last seven days). If you grant the separate actions permission, it can also archive a thread, move it back to your inbox, and create drafts. It never sends mail.

Where your data goes

FrontDesk keeps the threads it syncs, its rankings and your Google sign-in tokens on your computer, in a folder only your user account can read. It sends data to two places only: to Google, to read your mail and, if you allow it, to archive threads and save drafts; and to the AI model provider you configure, which receives the plain text of each thread so it can rank it and suggest a reply. Message formatting (HTML) and attachments are not sent to the model provider. That provider's own data terms apply to what it receives.

What it never does

FrontDesk does not sell, share or advertise with your data, does not use it to train models, and does not send it anywhere except the two places above.

Google API data

FrontDesk's use and transfer of information received from Google APIs to any other app adheres to the Google API Services User Data Policy, including the Limited Use requirements.

Keeping and deleting

Threads you archive, or that leave your inbox, are removed from FrontDesk's local store on the next sync. Deleting the ~/.frontdesk folder removes everything FrontDesk has stored. To revoke its access to your Google account, remove it at myaccount.google.com/permissions.
